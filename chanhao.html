A little bit about myself ...

2019 - 2023

When I graduated from secondary school, all I could think about was money. At the time, I believed the fastest way to earn it was through building a business and investing.

So I applied to study marketing, thinking it was the most essential skill for running a business, and I also taught myself stock trading on the side — which eventually led me to a stock trading assistant role at an investment bank.

2023

After graduating, I interned at a marketing agency, and what I saw there shook me. No matter how bad a company's product actually was, our job was to make it look good.

As a marketer, I had direct access to user reviews complaining about how poor the product was — and we were the ones tasked with burying them.

2024

So I moved on to my second plan: I used my self-taught stock trading experience to land a position as a stock trader assistant at Malacca Securities.

I joined right in 2024, at the exact moment of the Malaysia stock market crash. My days were spent staring at a monitor, waiting for the right entry point, while my mind kept wandering to all the other things I wanted to try and do. I ended up quitting during my probation period.

2025

After failing several times to find what I actually wanted, I became an insurance agent — a career I chose specifically to widen my perspective. I figured that if I talked to enough people, I'd eventually understand more about the world, and about myself. (You can find my entire journey as an insurance agent documented on my Xiaohongshu.)

Eventually, after talking to enough people, I arrived at a realization: life isn't just about making money, and money doesn't only come from a "high-paying career."

2026

That's when I gave myself a second chance to pursue what I actually love — programming. Back in secondary school, I loved math, I loved logic, and I loved design.

So I taught myself to code, starting with a simple personal dashboard, and eventually found 42 — which I joined knowing this time, I was committed to staying in this path long-term. And that's where I am now: learning to code at 42, with my sights set on becoming a front-end developer.
